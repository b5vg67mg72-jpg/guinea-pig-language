Chapter 1 · Paragraphs

Give every paragraph one clear job.

A paragraph is not simply a group of sentences. It develops one controlling idea. The topic sentence makes the claim; the middle explains and supports it; the final sentence shows why the point matters or connects forward.

MEAL paragraph

Main idea → Evidence → Analysis → Link

Model paragraph

Main idea: Regular low-stakes testing can improve long-term learning. Evidence: When students retrieve information from memory, they practise the mental process required in later assessment. Analysis: This differs from rereading, which may create familiarity without confirming that knowledge can be recalled independently. Link: Therefore, short retrieval activities can function as learning tools rather than merely as measures of performance.

Try it

Write a MEAL paragraph supporting this claim: “Universities should provide more quiet study spaces.” Include one concrete example as evidence.

Chapter 2 · Evidence

Introduce, use, and explain evidence.

Evidence never speaks for itself. A quotation, statistic, or research finding needs a frame: who produced it, what it shows, and how it supports your argument.

Evidence sandwich

Introduce the source → present a quotation/paraphrase/data point → analyse its significance

Source note

A hypothetical campus survey finds that 68% of commuting students report difficulty attending early-morning support sessions.

Weak use

“68% of commuting students report difficulty.” This proves support is bad.

Stronger use

A campus survey found that 68% of commuting students experienced difficulty attending early-morning support sessions. This pattern suggests that the timing of support, rather than lack of student interest, may restrict access for learners who travel longer distances.

Paraphrase without patchwriting

  1. Read until you understand.
  2. Look away from the source.
  3. Write the idea in your own sentence structure.
  4. Compare for accuracy.
  5. Cite the source even though the words are yours.
Try it

Paraphrase the source note above and add one sentence explaining why the result matters. Use the citation conventions required by your school.

Chapter 3 · Essays

Build an argument the reader can follow.

A thesis is more than a topic. It states the central position or interpretation that the essay will develop. Each body paragraph should then make a distinct contribution to that thesis.

Topic only

This essay discusses online learning.

Arguable thesis

Although online learning expands access, universities must combine flexibility with structured interaction and technical support if digital courses are to produce equitable outcomes.

Essay map

Introduction: context + problem + thesis
Body 1: first reason or dimension
Body 2: second reason or complication
Body 3: counterargument, limitation, or implication
Conclusion: synthesise the argument; do not merely repeat it

Planning task

Question: Should attendance be compulsory in university seminars? Write a one-sentence thesis and three body-paragraph claims. Make sure the claims are different but all support the thesis.

Possible direction

Thesis: Seminar attendance should normally be expected because discussion is part of the learning process, but policies should allow documented flexibility for students facing health, work, or caring constraints.
Body ideas: collaborative value · accountability and preparation · fair exceptions.

Chapter 4 · Style

Sound precise, not artificially complicated.

Academic style values clarity, accuracy, and appropriate caution. Long words do not automatically create strong writing. Choose the most exact word and make the relationship between ideas visible.

Overstated

Social media always destroys students’ concentration.

Appropriately cautious

Frequent social-media interruptions may reduce sustained attention during some study tasks.

Useful cautious language

may · can · tends to · appears to · is associated with · in some contexts · the evidence suggests

Create cohesion

Use repeated key concepts, clear pronouns, and logical connectors. Do not add connectors mechanically. However must introduce a contrast; therefore must introduce a result.

Improve these sentences

1. Technology is good and it helps everything.
2. Many people study online. However, online enrolment has increased.
3. This proves that every student prefers digital books.

Possible revisions

1. Digital tools can improve access to course materials and feedback.
2. Many people study online; consequently, online enrolment has increased. (Use only if the first fact causes the second.)
3. This finding suggests that some students may prefer digital books.

Chapter 5 · Revision

Revise ideas before correcting commas.

Revision and proofreading are different. Revision improves argument, organisation, evidence, and clarity. Proofreading checks surface errors after the ideas are stable.

Three-pass method
  1. Argument pass: thesis, paragraph purpose, evidence, missing logic.
  2. Clarity pass: sentence focus, transitions, unnecessary repetition.
  3. Accuracy pass: grammar, punctuation, spelling, formatting, citations.
Before

There are many reasons why libraries are important and this is something universities should think about because students use them and they are quiet and there are resources which helps learning.

After

University libraries support learning by providing quiet workspaces, specialist resources, and reliable access to academic materials.

Try it

Revise the “before” sentence yourself. Then compare your version with the model—not for identical wording, but for focus, precision, and economy.

Reusable tool

Academic writing checklist

  • My introduction leads to a specific, arguable thesis.
  • Each body paragraph has one clear purpose.
  • I explain how evidence supports my claim.
  • I distinguish my ideas from source ideas and cite appropriately.
  • I use cautious language when the evidence is limited.
  • Transitions describe real logical relationships.
  • My conclusion synthesises rather than copies the introduction.
  • I revised the argument before proofreading sentences.
  • I followed my institution’s citation and integrity rules.
